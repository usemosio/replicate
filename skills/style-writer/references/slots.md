# Filling the slots

Write for an agent that has never seen the references and will build a new video from your words and numbers alone.
Concrete beats evocative: "rim light 4–6 px, warm orange, on the side facing the fire" builds; "moody lighting"
does not.

## description (skill only)

One sentence that triggers the skill: what it makes, then when to use it, with the words a person would type.
"Make narrated story shorts in a puppet low-poly look: one-word karaoke captions, slow push-ins, warm firelit
sets. Use for narrated stories, explainers told as a tale, or when asked for this look." Max 1024 characters, no
reference titles, channels or people.

## summary

What the style is, what it is good for, and the feeling it leaves, in three or four sentences.

## rhythm

What drives the cuts (the voice, the beat, an action, a reveal), how long the holds are compared with the measured
median, where it speeds up or breathes, and how the first three seconds hook.

## camera

The language behind the camera table: which shot types get which move (wide = slow push, close-up = static), how a
move starts and lands (the measured ease), how far it goes, and what never moves.

## look

The rendering style (flat vector, cel, low-poly 3D, collage, live action, screen recording), light (key direction,
rim, shadows, flicker), shading and texture, depth (layers, parallax, blur), and colour *roles*: which palette entry
is the backdrop, the mid-tone, the accent, the text. A new brand fills the roles; the roles stay.

## characters and objects

The drawing system, not the characters: proportions (head to body), outline weight, how faces are built, how limbs
bend, idle motion, how speech shows. "None" when the style has none.

## type and captions

Traits of the type (weight, case, width, outline or shadow, size relative to the frame), a redistributable family
that matches them, where text sits, how a caption group pops in and changes, and the lit-word treatment.

## motion grammar

How things enter and exit (direction, overshoot, scale, fade), the eases and durations, secondary motion (squash,
follow-through, boil), loops that keep a still scene alive.

## transitions

Each transition the references use and how it is built over its frames: hard cut, a flash hiding a cut, a push
into a cut, whip, wipe, match cut.

## sound

Voice (pace, tone, how close), music (genre, level under voice), effects (which events get one), and how picture
and sound line up (cuts on words, beats or neither). The measured loudness is the target level.

## make it yours

What a new video swaps in: topic, copy, characters, brand colours (mapped to the colour roles), logo, footage. What
stays: rhythm, camera, motion grammar, type treatment, light.

## keep / never copy

What must stay for the result to read as this style (the two or three things a viewer recognises first), and what
must never be taken from the references: their footage, characters, logos, music, voices and words.

## extra rules (skill only)

Anything specific a builder would get wrong: a minimum shot length, a caption limit per group, a colour that must
never be the backdrop. Delete the slot line when there is nothing.
